Investment Proposals

IP Expectations

The Investment Proposal process is the core research cycle for QR members. Every semester you are expected to develop and present 1–2 strategies through this structured pipeline.

Quick Facts

IP cycles run twice per semester. Each QR member works on 1–2 strategies per cycle. Final presentations go to the full leadership team.

The Four Phases
Phase 01
Team Formation & Proposal
Brainstorm strategies, outline conceptual rationale and data requirements, select one for development.
Phase 02
Development & Testing
~3 weeks. Acquire data via data-ngin, code strategy logic, run backtests with trade-ngin. Analyze Sharpe, drawdown, and trading costs.
Phase 03
Reporting & Review
Compile a detailed research report (theory, implementation, backtest results, risk analysis). Present to leadership.
Phase 04
Incubation & Deployment
Approved strategies run in paper trading. Monitor real-time performance. Strong results graduate to the experimental portfolio.
Minimum Standards
Sharpe Ratio
> 0.8
Net of realistic transaction costs. Do not report gross of fees.
Max Drawdown
< 30%
Measured over the full backtest window, not cherry-picked periods.
Annualized Volatility
12–20%
Target band per instrument, per volatility-adjusted position sizing.
What Gets Evaluated
Economic Rationale
Strategies must have a sound economic or behavioral foundation — not pure data mining. Explain why the inefficiency exists and why it should persist. Reference behavioral biases, structural market phenomena, or risk premia.
Rigor & Methodology
Proper out-of-sample testing, walk-forward validation, realistic slippage and commission assumptions. No look-ahead bias. Results should hold up under parameter perturbation (sensitivity analysis).
Portfolio Fit
Show how the strategy decorrelates with existing live strategies. A lower Sharpe strategy can still be valuable if it provides genuine diversification to the overall portfolio.
Creativity & Innovation
We want novel approaches — new data sources, unconventional signal construction, creative risk management. Incremental tweaks to existing ideas require strong justification.
Do Not

Submit strategies that are pure data-mined curve fits. Do not cherry-pick backtest windows. Do not ignore transaction costs. Do not re-submit a previously rejected IP without substantial changes.

What Your Report Must Include
  • Executive summary (1 page) — strategy concept, key metrics, portfolio fit
  • Economic/theoretical rationale — cited literature or first-principles reasoning
  • Data sources, cleaning process, and any data limitations or survivorship bias considerations
  • Signal construction methodology (full formulas, all parameters)
  • Position sizing and risk management rules
  • Full backtest results with equity curve, Sharpe, drawdown, Calmar ratio, monthly return table
  • Out-of-sample or walk-forward validation results
  • Sensitivity / robustness analysis across parameter ranges
  • Portfolio correlation analysis against current live strategies
  • Honest limitations and failure modes section

80 in 8 — Mental Math Speed Drill
What It Is

80 arithmetic/quant questions in 8 minutes. Used by Jane Street, Citadel, and most prop shops as a screening tool. Target: 70+ correct. Practice until you can do basic arithmetic without hesitation.

Category
Arithmetic Speed
Mental multiplication/division, powers of 2 (up to 2¹⁶), squares up to 30², percentage calculations (e.g., 17.5% of 840), fractions to decimals (1/7, 1/9, 1/13 etc.)

Practice: 347 × 8, 1024 / 16, 2¹⁰, 23², 14.5% of 3,200
Category
Fraction & Root Estimation
Estimate square roots to 1 decimal, fraction arithmetic under time pressure, compound interest approximations (Rule of 72).

Practice: √170 ≈ ?, √520 ≈ ?, 3/7 + 5/11, how long to double at 6%?
Probability Classics

These questions appear constantly. Know the setups cold — not just the answers but the reasoning chain.

The Monty Hall Problem — You pick door 1. Host opens door 3 (goat). Switch or stay?
Switch. Staying wins 1/3 of the time. Switching wins 2/3 of the time. The host's action is not random — he always opens a goat door, which concentrates the remaining 2/3 probability onto the other door. Intuition fails here; condition on the host's strategy.
Birthday Problem — How many people needed for >50% chance two share a birthday?
23 people. P(all different) = 365/365 × 364/365 × 363/365 × ... After 23 terms, this product drops below 0.5. The counterintuitive answer comes from the quadratic number of pairs: 23 people = 253 pairs, each with a ~0.27% collision chance.
Dice: Roll two fair dice. Given the sum is at least 7, what's the probability the sum is exactly 7?
6/21. P(sum ≥ 7) = 21/36. P(sum = 7) = 6/36. Conditional probability: (6/36)/(21/36) = 6/21 ≈ 28.6%.
Bayes: A test is 99% accurate. Disease prevalence is 0.1%. You test positive. What's the probability you have the disease?
≈ 9%. P(disease|positive) = P(positive|disease)·P(disease) / P(positive). Numerator: 0.99 × 0.001 = 0.00099. Denominator: 0.99×0.001 + 0.01×0.999 ≈ 0.01098. Result: 0.00099/0.01098 ≈ 9%. Base rate matters enormously — this is the core lesson for risk management too.
Expected Value: A fair coin. Win $2 on heads, lose $1 on tails. What's the EV per flip?
$0.50. EV = 0.5 × $2 + 0.5 × (−$1) = $1 − $0.50 = $0.50. Simple, but interview pressure causes mistakes. Always write out the full formula.
Conditional: You draw 2 cards from a standard deck without replacement. Given the first is an Ace, what's P(second is also an Ace)?
3/51 = 1/17. After drawing one Ace, 3 Aces remain among 51 cards. Conditional probability on the observed event (first is Ace).
Random Walk: A drunk man starts at position 0. Each step he goes +1 or −1 with equal probability. What's the expected number of steps to reach ±N?
N². The expected first passage time from 0 to ±N is N². This is the optional stopping theorem result for symmetric random walks. Key insight for understanding how long drawdowns can persist in mean-zero strategies.
St. Petersburg Paradox: A game pays $2^n where n is flips until heads. What's a fair price to play?
EV is infinite, but no rational person pays more than ~$20. EV = Σ (1/2ⁿ)·2ⁿ = Σ 1 = ∞. The resolution is utility — logarithmic utility makes the game worth a finite price. This underpins Kelly criterion and why we use log utility in position sizing.
Sequences & Patterns

Pattern recognition under time pressure. State the next two terms and the rule.

1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, __?
21, 34. Fibonacci sequence: each term = sum of previous two. Appears in option pricing (Fibonacci retracements), though the market application is mostly behavioral.
2, 6, 12, 20, 30, 42, __?
56, 72. n(n+1): 1×2, 2×3, 3×4, ... The nth term is n(n+1). Differences are 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 — arithmetic progression of differences.
1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, __?
49, 64. Perfect squares. Know these instantly up to 30²=900.
3, 6, 11, 18, 27, 38, __?
51, 66. n²+2: 1²+2, 2²+2, 3²+2, ... Differences: 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13 — odd numbers. Second differences are constant (2).
1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, __? (and what's the 20th term?)
64, 128. Powers of 2. 20th term = 2¹⁹ = 524,288. Know powers of 2 through 2²⁰ = 1,048,576.
Brain Teasers
You have 12 balls, one is heavier or lighter. Find the odd ball in 3 weighings.
Divide into groups of 4. Weigh 4 vs 4. If balanced, odd ball is in the remaining 4. If unbalanced, it's in one of the weighed groups (and you know whether it's heavier or lighter relative to the other). Use remaining weighings to binary search within the suspect group. The key insight: each weighing gives 3 outcomes (left heavy, right heavy, balanced), so 3 weighings = 3³ = 27 distinguishable outcomes — enough for 24 possibilities (12 balls × 2 directions).
How many piano tuners are in Chicago?
~100–200. Fermi estimation: Chicago pop ~2.7M → ~1M households → ~400k have pianos (rough 40% guess, likely lower ~15% → 150k pianos). Pianos need tuning ~1x/year. A tuner does ~4 pianos/day × 250 days = 1,000 pianos/year. 150k ÷ 1,000 ≈ 150 tuners. The method matters more than the number — show your decomposition chain clearly.
You're in a dark room with 100 socks: 50 red, 50 blue. Minimum picks to guarantee a matching pair?
3. With 3 picks, by pigeonhole principle, at least two must be the same color. (Worst case: first two are one of each color; third must match one.)
A lily pad doubles in size every day. It takes 100 days to cover the pond. When was the pond half-covered?
Day 99. If it doubles daily and covers the full pond on day 100, it covered half the pond the day before. Exponential growth — the answer is always one step back from the end.
You have a 3-gallon and 5-gallon jug. How do you measure exactly 4 gallons?
Fill 5-gal. Pour into 3-gal until full. 5-gal now has 2 gal. Empty 3-gal. Pour 2 gal from 5-gal into 3-gal. Fill 5-gal again. Pour from 5-gal into 3-gal (needs 1 more gal to fill). 5-gal now has exactly 4 gal. Classic water-pouring puzzle. Used to test systematic thinking.
Market Microstructure Essentials
Bid-Ask Spread
The spread is the market maker's compensation for providing liquidity and bearing inventory risk. Spread = 2 × (adverse selection cost + inventory cost + operational cost). Wider spreads on illiquid, high-volatility instruments.
Market Impact
Large orders move price against you. Impact ≈ σ × √(Q/ADV) where Q is order size, ADV is average daily volume. Square-root law: doubling order size doesn't double impact — it increases by √2. Critical for position sizing at scale.
Adverse Selection
Informed traders systematically trade against market makers. The spread compensates for the risk of trading with someone who knows more. Kyle's Lambda measures price impact per unit of order flow. High Lambda = high information asymmetry.
TWAP vs VWAP Execution
TWAP slices orders evenly over time. VWAP weights execution by expected volume profile (more trading when volume is high). VWAP reduces market impact on average but creates tracking error risk. Most institutional desks target VWAP.

Legal & Compliance

Legal Framework

AlgoGators is structured to protect its members while maintaining operational flexibility. Every member must understand and comply with these principles.

Core Disclaimer — Always Apply

AlgoGators' activities are for educational purposes only and do not constitute investment advice. Do not represent our signals or research as investment recommendations to any external party.

Legal Structure
Non-Profit Corporation
AlgoGators is incorporated as a non-profit corporation at the state level. We do not hold federal non-profit (501c3) status. This structure allows focus on our educational mission while maintaining organizational independence from the university.
Liability Protection
The non-profit corporate structure shields individual members from personal liability for fund-related risks and losses. You are a member of an incorporated entity — not personally liable for fund operations, provided you act within your defined role.
Board of Directors
Governance is overseen by the Board of Directors, which currently includes Dustin Hebrank and Patrick Bott. The Board provides strategic guidance, reviews major decisions, and ensures adherence to the fund's mission.
Operational Focus
All fund activities and resources must align with our educational objectives. This is not a loophole — it is a genuine organizational commitment. Decisions that stray from the educational mission require Board approval.
Participation Agreement

Every member of AlgoGators is required to sign a Participation Agreement before accessing fund systems, data, or strategies. The agreement covers:

  • Member roles and responsibilities within the fund
  • Intellectual property assignment — all research, strategies, and code developed as part of AlgoGators are IP of the fund
  • Confidentiality obligations — live strategy parameters and portfolio positions are confidential
  • Compliance with the fund's educational mission and non-investment-advice policy
  • Data usage restrictions — market data provided by Databento may only be used for AlgoGators research purposes
Where to Sign

Participation Agreements are administered by the President at onboarding. If you have not signed, contact the President immediately. You should not have access to live systems without a signed agreement on file.
